# Review a defined body of literature

1. Freeze the question and scope before searching. Specify population or context, method or phenomenon, comparison, outcomes, dates, languages, and publication types.
2. Record databases, exact query strings, filters, and timestamps. Use arXiv or scholarly metadata for discovery, not as a substitute for full text.
3. Register candidate records and deduplicate versions. Link papers that report the same underlying study. Do not count them as independent experiments.
4. Screen titles and abstracts against recorded eligibility criteria. Keep inclusion decisions and reasons. Unavailable full text is an access gap, not a negative finding.
5. Assess full texts. Record exclusions with a specific reason. Keep counts for identified records, duplicates, screened records, full texts assessed, and included studies. Reconcile the counts.
6. Extract task, data, baseline, sample, seeds, compute, metrics, uncertainty, funding/conflicts, and limitations. Use “not reported” rather than guessing missing fields.
7. Assess bias and applicability at study level. Check whether baselines, tuning effort, hardware, and test conditions are comparable.
8. Synthesize only comparable outcomes. Do not average unrelated benchmark scores or count positive papers as votes. Include negative and conflicting results.
9. Search references and later citations within the cap. Log this additional route. Check revisions, corrections, and retractions.
10. Report search limits, unavailable texts, protocol changes, and whether screening or extraction received independent review.

Deliver the answer, eligibility rules, search log, reconciled selection counts, extraction table with locators, bias/applicability notes, contrary findings, and open questions.

Stop at the approved cap. Report incomplete screening and the unassessed count. A bounded review may use the research workflow's saturation rule, but cannot claim exhaustive discovery from saturation alone.

Call this procedure PRISMA-inspired. Do not call it PRISMA-compliant, a systematic review, or a meta-analysis without the relevant protocol, reporting, and methodological work.

Basis: PRISMA 2020 checklist and the research evaluation design. See `../../sources.md`.
