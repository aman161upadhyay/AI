# Positioning brief

## Inputs
Request product capabilities, actual alternatives, buyer/user distinction, permissioned interviews and approved proof. Ask for material gaps. Treat the task as a draft, not publication.

## Procedure
1. List alternatives, including manual work and doing nothing, with evidence IDs.
2. Compare capabilities that are demonstrated against those alternatives. Mark unproven differentiation.
3. Translate supported capabilities into value for the buying situation. Do not infer time savings.
4. Define the best-fit segment and exclusions from evidence. Label inferred segment choices as hypotheses.
5. Propose a category and one positioning statement. State what evidence would reverse the choice.
6. List the largest objection and a research question that would test it.

## Output
Return buyer and situation; alternatives table; capability-to-value-to-proof table; segment and exclusions; category and positioning statement; unknowns; approval request.

## Checks and stop rules
Every factual claim has a source and observation date. Every quote has permission. Missing customer evidence becomes an interview plan. Stop before publishing, exporting contacts or collecting identifiable data without approval.
