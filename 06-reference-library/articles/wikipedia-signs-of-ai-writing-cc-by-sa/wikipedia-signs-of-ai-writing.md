## Scope and caveats

The page is a descriptive community field guide, not Wikipedia policy. A cue can appear in human writing. Familiarity with a model's habits does not make a reader a reliable detector. Citation verification and content quality matter more than an accusation about origin.

The examples below are new illustrations of the categories, not copied incidents from the source. They are fictional and supply no facts for production drafts.

## Content

| Category | What the guide describes | Illustrative example and editing response |
|---|---|---|
| Undue significance | Ordinary facts presented as evidence of broad historical importance. | "The office opening changed the industry." Require evidence of the claimed change. |
| Canned notability and media coverage | Lists of prestigious outlets substituted for the substance of coverage. | "Covered by major newspapers." Identify what the source actually reported. |
| Superficial analysis | Participial additions that imply a significance unsupported by the source. | "The bridge opened in June, symbolizing unity." Keep the date; require support for symbolism. |
| Promotional wording | Praise replacing description of a place, product, or person. | "An unmatched destination." Replace with sourced features or remove it. |
| Vague connection | Relationships left ambiguous despite an available precise role. | "Connected with the institute." State founder or employee only if verified. |
| Vague attribution | Unnamed experts and generalized opinions. | "Observers agree." Name the source and scope of the view. |
| Stock challenges and outlook | Predictable positive endings that add no verified plan. | "The future remains bright." End on the remaining factual issue. |
| Awards and recognition | Formulaic sections used to imply importance. | An award list without relevant sourcing needs verification, not extra praise. |

## Language and grammar

The guide discusses clusters of model-favored vocabulary, avoidance of basic forms of "is" and "has," and negative parallel constructions. It also notes leads that treat broad article titles as proper names and lists forced into three-part rhythms.

"The museum serves as a home for the collection" may be clearer as "The museum houses the collection." A three-item list is valid when there are three distinct facts. A real contrast should stay when it corrects an actual misconception. No single word establishes origin.

## Style

Categories include title case, excessive boldface, inline-header lists, heading errors, emoji used as formatting, unusual tables, curly quotation marks, and highlighted text. Heading problems include repeated title headings, skipped levels, excessive top-level headings, and headings with no content except more headings.

For example, a heading above every sentence of a short reply adds friction. A long technical reference may need headings and tables. Curly quotes may come from a text editor. Markdown itself is normal on GitHub, though unexpected Markdown inside wikitext can be residue.

## Communication and markup residue

The page covers assistant-to-user comments, offers of further help, disclaimers about source availability, unfilled templates, and markup irregularities. Its dated disclaimer examples reflect changing model behavior.

"Here is the article you requested" belongs to a conversation, not an encyclopedia article. A visible author-name field should block publication until completed. Do not remove a genuine source limitation merely because some model disclaimers are formulaic.

## Citations

The guide distinguishes broken external links, invalid DOI or ISBN strings, identifiers that resolve to unrelated works, book citations without usable location details, unconventional reference syntax, tracking parameters, links to searches, and unused named references.

A DOI that resolves is insufficient: confirm title, author, date, and the supported claim. Search links do not substitute for the cited document. Removing citation residue without verifying the underlying evidence leaves the main error intact.

## Comments and editing process

Comment-specific indicators include formulaic edit summaries, assurances of policy compliance, repeated claims that information was preserved, overemphasis on citation reliability or template details, and references to article-review processes.

An edit summary such as "All policies followed and all information preserved" is not evidence that either check happened. Review the change itself.

## Miscellaneous and historical categories

The page discusses abrupt changes in style, submission statements, pre-placed maintenance templates, canned user pages, permissions gaming, differences between models, and content biases, including pro-authoritarian bias. These are contextual observations, not a reliable political or authorship classifier.

It also discusses indicators of human writing, ineffective indicators, and historical signs. Text age relative to ChatGPT's launch and an author's ability to explain editorial choices provide context, not universal proof about all possible AI systems.

Historical indicators include knowledge-cutoff statements from 2022 to 2023, section summaries, refusals, abrupt cutoffs, outdated access dates, and excessive synonym variation. The 2026-10-25 snapshot places em dash overuse in a historical period ending September 2026. A fixed list can therefore outlive the behavior it describes.

## What the writing agent takes

Review unsupported significance and source defects before replacing phrases. Preserve genre-specific formatting when useful. Use the catalogue as a queue of questions, never as an authorship verdict. Keep a date and exceptions with each rule. Verify citations after every rewrite.
