## Summary

Graham recommends spoken language for prose intended to be understood. Formal sentence shapes can make a simple thought seem more substantial than it is. Technical subjects still need technical terms, but the grammar need not become ceremonial.

His sentence test is: "Is this the way I'd say this if I were talking to a friend?" He describes reading essays aloud before publication. When sentence-level editing cannot rescue a stiff draft, explain its argument orally and rebuild from that explanation.

"You don't need complex sentences to express complex ideas."

These are practitioner judgments, not controlled comprehension results. Graham explicitly allows exceptions, including poetry. His numerical flourish about how many writers this would outperform is not used as evidence here.

## What the writing agent takes

Read the final draft aloud. Ask Aman to dictate a sentence that still feels unlike him. Preserve specialized vocabulary when the audience needs it. Do not confuse conversational syntax with invented intimacy or slang.
