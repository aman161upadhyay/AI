# Politics and the English Language: six rules

Author: George Orwell.
URL: https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language
Date: April 1946. Accessed 2026-10-25.
Licence note: Paraphrased summary. Copyright status varies by jurisdiction; no full text is reproduced or universal public-domain status asserted.

## Summary

Orwell links unclear language with evasive thinking. His six rules, paraphrased, are:

1. Avoid stale figures of speech.
2. Choose a shorter word when it carries the meaning.
3. Remove dispensable words.
4. Prefer active voice when it identifies the action clearly.
5. Prefer ordinary English to jargon or foreign phrases when it works.
6. Break the other rules rather than produce bad prose.

The final rule prevents mechanical enforcement. Passive voice can be correct when the actor is unknown or irrelevant. Necessary technical terms can be more precise than ordinary substitutes.

## What the writing agent takes

Restore actors only when evidence supplies them. Replace abstract evaluation with a supported observation. Do not shorten away qualifications or helpful grammar. Use the rules to inspect a sentence, not to punish every passive construction.
