# Delving into LLM-assisted writing in biomedical publications through excess vocabulary

Authors: Dmitry Kobak, Rita González-Márquez, Emőke-Ágnes Horvát, Jan Lause.
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07016
Date: First preprint June 2024; journal version 2025-07-02. Accessed 2026-10-25.
Licence note: Original summary of inspected research. No figures or substantial source text reproduced; no licence for the full paper is asserted.

## Summary

The updated study analyzes more than 15 million PubMed abstracts from 2010 to 2024. It identifies 379 unusually elevated style words and estimates a lower bound of 13.5% LLM processing in 2024.

The method examines vocabulary discontinuities relative to earlier trends without requiring labeled human and AI training documents. Abstracts without marker words do not contribute to the lower-bound estimate. Human adoption of model-associated vocabulary can complicate attribution.

Earlier versions use different titles and estimates. The number above refers to the updated result, not every version of arXiv:2406.07016.

## What the writing agent takes

Vocabulary patterns can motivate editing review at corpus scale. They cannot convict an individual author or establish misconduct. Keep year, denominator, version, and lower-bound language with the estimate. Do not compare it directly with Liang's sentence-level estimate.
